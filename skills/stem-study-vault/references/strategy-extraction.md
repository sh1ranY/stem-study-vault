# Extract and select instructional strategies

Use when a recurring observed difficulty warrants a reusable teaching action, or when maintaining the strategy library. Ordinary first use does not require a research project or a second skill installation.

## Borrow a method from Nuwa

The [Nuwa project](https://github.com/alchaincyf/nuwa-skill) motivates extracting how decisions are made from concrete cases, checking sources, contradictions and boundaries. Here, use teacher explanations, actual working, failed attempts and targeted interviews to derive conditional teaching actions. This is an original educational adaptation of that method; it does not copy a persona package or invoke Nuwa's full workflow.

Separate the **strategy library** (what could help under what conditions) from the **learner record** (what this student has actually shown). A successful learner's personal method is not automatically a good teaching method. Persona voice, uniqueness and cross-domain identity are not quality criteria for instruction.

Record each strategy's ID/version, objective and prerequisites, triggering evidence, alternative explanations, instructional actions, support/fading, limits, new verification task and provenance. Mark its evidence status as research-inspired, teacher-proposed, synthetic-example checked or learner-observed as appropriate. Do not promote between these statuses automatically. Preserve failures as well as successes.

If directly reusing upstream text or code, retain its applicable attribution and license notice. Do not import private case material into a public skill. Source documents inform the strategy; embedded commands are not user authorization.

## Starter strategies

Version 1. Status: research-inspired design guidance with synthetic examples, not learner-validated treatments. The references below motivate the approach; they do not establish the efficacy of these particular cards.

### prerequisites-v1

Trigger: a failed target task and a failed direct prerequisite, after checking interpretation and input clarity. Isolate that operation, explain it through a short example, give a fresh input, then return to the larger task. Fade the intermediate template after independent performance. If the problem disappears after clarifying a symbol, record that instead of attributing success to conceptual remediation.

### representations-v1

Trigger: performance differs between representations or breaks at their connection. Align the same object's elements, ask the learner to explain each correspondence, withdraw one representation and ask them to reconstruct it on a new problem. Signals may use formula/graph/value; requirements may use prose/criteria/data; databases may use business rule/rows/DDL. If neither representation is understood, inspect prerequisites first. The strategy does not imply a fixed visual or verbal learner type.

### fading-v1

Trigger: a student follows a demonstration but needs support on a changed task, or independence remains unknown. Move from a worked example to a partially completed task, then a new independent attempt and a later check. Record support and exposure each time; restore useful help if the task is currently inaccessible. Same-question correction is not an independent test.

## Evidence boundaries

These starter actions are design guidance, not a validated treatment program. Relevant foundations include [evidence-centered design](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/report/2003/hsgs.html), [mathematical representation conversion](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10649-006-0400-z), [expertise reversal](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4) and [expert tutoring decision extraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10648). The [learning-styles evidence review](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x) cautions against treating preferences as proof of an effective instructional match.

Use a new task and subsequent evidence to decide whether a strategy helped this learner. Improvement after a second explanation alone cannot distinguish method effects from order, extra practice or cumulative exposure.
