# Diagnose a learning need and change the teaching

Use for optional calibration, submitted attempts and targeted course updates. A diagnosis is a provisional explanation of performance on a specific objective, not an assessment of general intelligence, attitude or a fixed learning style.

## A short first encounter

Inspect current-unit sources, terminology and prerequisites before selecting tasks. Reuse known goals, language and prior attempts. A few short tasks are usually enough to identify a useful starting point; neither a fixed duration nor answering every dimension is required.

Let the learner attempt in their normal format, including paper, diagrams or prose. Retain the original response and reasoning. If an image is ambiguous, clarify the uncertain symbol before grading. Use a targeted follow-up to distinguish alternative explanations of an error. Do not force continuous think-aloud when a short retrospective question would suffice.

When help is needed, offer a general cue, a specific step, or a worked demonstration according to the difficulty. Record the actual support used, including answer exposure. A successful correction on the same question is supported performance; verify independence on a new comparable question. Do not use a reserved exam as diagnostic material.

Offer a skip route. With no attempt, use the stated background and existing teaching defaults, mark readiness unassessed and continue the authorized build. Missing replies do not justify inventing results or repeatedly blocking construction. For a whole course, calibrate the current batch rather than testing every future topic first.

## Six observation dimensions

| Dimension | Evidence to seek | Teaching consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prerequisites | A direct prerequisite operation, with working | Add a short bridge at the first missing operation; skip redundant drills when supported |
| Concepts and representations | Conditions, counterexamples, explanations and conversions between representations | Connect the specific representations or clarify the distinction |
| Response to support | What changed after a recorded cue or example | Adjust explanation granularity and fade the help on a new task |
| Transfer and retention | Unseen variation, changed conditions, later independent attempts | Schedule variation and a later check; retain unknowns until observed |
| Self-assessment and methods | Pre-feedback confidence, uncertain step, checking and revision | Practice explanation, prediction and checking; avoid conclusions from one confidence report |
| Goals, conditions and preferences | Learner-provided goal, language, time and interaction needs | Adapt pacing and presentation without claiming this proves a learning-style match |

This is a design framework, not a validated six-factor scale. Keep dimensions course/objective-specific and leave unobserved parts unknown. Time taken, skipping or requesting help may reflect context or access difficulties; they do not by themselves measure attitude. Verbal ability, algebraic manipulation, spatial reasoning and visual preference are different observations.

## The decision must change something concrete

For each material adjustment, record: **objective → observed evidence → provisional explanation and alternatives → teaching change → new verification task**. A longer generic lesson is not enough.

Examples:

- A student substitutes a shifted ramp correctly but predicts zero after two ramps cancel. Check a numerical point to distinguish omitted constants from confusing slope with height. If supported, teach the plateau with a piecewise subtraction and a diagram, then ask for a new waveform.
- A student classifies requirements correctly but substitutes an average for an “every request” limit. Use a counterexample and preserve the customer's stated conditions, then ask for a different scenario. Do not reteach every classification by default.
- A student treats a composite key as making each column unique. Contrast allowed rows, link them to the business rule and DDL, then move to a new relationship. Do not diagnose SQL syntax from a conceptual answer alone.

Use [strategy-extraction.md](strategy-extraction.md) for reusable strategies and [subject-design.md](subject-design.md) for task design. Preserve common content goals and rigor; a weak representation is often something to develop, not avoid permanently.

## Follow-up and corrections

Keep initial answers, supported corrections and new attempts distinct. Give the learner a short understandable summary of the evidence, the tentative interpretation and the next action. Let them correct OCR, explain an interruption or dispute a judgment. Retire affected inferences and configurations when their evidence is corrected; retain a traceable correction rather than silently changing the original response.

One correct new task supports a narrow observation, not mastery of an entire chapter. Delayed retention needs an actual later attempt, not a scheduled question. If the task was seen, label the exposure and use another task when independence matters. Arrange later checks in the learning route; do not create background reminders unless requested.

Update only affected generated units and future tasks. Preserve personal notes and source coverage. When the learner still struggles, revisit the hypothesis or the prerequisite; the workflow must allow no improvement, not force a success narrative.
