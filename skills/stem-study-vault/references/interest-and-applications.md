# Interest, applications and cross-course bridges

Read when designing an interest-led unit, a professional application, a prerequisite bridge or a multimedia activity. Use with [teaching.md](teaching.md) for depth and [subject-design.md](subject-design.md) for tasks. These are teaching design rules, not a claim that any activity or personalization method has proved effective.

## Give the learner a reason to investigate

Start from a phenomenon or decision the learner can care about and can meaningfully affect or explain. Connect it to explicit course objectives and state the limited capability the unit is intended to develop. Offer a route directly to the common explanation or exam practice; interest-led study does not replace either route.

A useful activity often connects **prediction → observation or operation → explanation → changed task**. Adapt the order and form to the subject; do not require a simulator, video or prescribed number of steps in every lesson. When a prediction matters, ask before revealing the observation. Let learners revisit or explore freely afterward and label that exposure when checking independence. A slider movement is an interaction, not a correct scientific explanation.

Choose a small number of consequential variables. Tell the learner what stays fixed, what to compare, and what result would challenge their explanation. End with an interpretable result and a worthwhile next question, while providing a natural completion point. Do not use dwell time, endless tasks or novelty alone as measures of learning value.

## Share the core; vary the application and support

Keep the core definitions, conditions and necessary reasoning available to everyone. Surround them with optional phenomenon entry points, local prerequisite refreshers, fuller derivations and further applications. A shorter route can leave deeper objectives unassessed; it must not silently rename an omitted requirement as mastered or fully taught.

Professional background can suggest an application, but learners may choose any relevant lens. Vary the actual decision, variables and constraints: an algorithm task might examine boundary handling and computational cost, while a sensing task examines measurement noise and sampling. Do not infer interests or competence from a nationality or degree title.

Treat prerequisite support, representation, depth and help level as separate choices. A learner can use a visual refresher and then reconstruct an algebraic derivation. A preference can guide the first presentation; subsequent adjustments need specific evidence and another check. Preserve the distinction between declared interests, observed performance and tentative recommendations in [learning-diagnosis.md](learning-diagnosis.md).

## Make each cross-course connection useful where it is needed

Name the relation and explain the part used in the current step:

| Relation | What the link must establish |
| --- | --- |
| Necessary prerequisite | The operation or concept needed now, with a short check or refresher |
| Derivation basis | The actual premise or equality that justifies a transition |
| Shared structure | What is shared, and the differing objects, assumptions or conditions |
| Application | How a concept helps make a decision with stated practical constraints |
| Analogy | What the comparison explains and where it stops being valid |

Link to the actual teaching passage and provide a return point to the original problem. A bridge should reactivate the required idea, offer enough explanation and a small check, then reconnect it to the step that prompted it. If the destination is absent, supply the necessary local explanation or explicitly leave the dependency unresolved; do not create a decorative broken link. Do not invent an official prerequisite, past course or week from a plausible mathematical relationship.

For example, Euler's formula can justify converting complex exponentials to sine and cosine, but that alone does not teach Fourier decomposition: coefficient extraction, orthogonality and relevant sum or integral conditions still need their own explanation. An engineering application shared with a database course does not make signal theory a prerequisite for SQL.

## Give media a teaching job and a reliable alternative

Use a static diagram to compare states, interaction to investigate a variable, video to inspect a real process, and text or equations for precise reasoning. State what to attend to. Video needs useful timestamps and captions or a readable equivalent; an external video alone cannot supply an otherwise missing required explanation.

Generate scientific curves, spectra, filter results and other quantitative diagrams from a defined calculation or verified construction. Independently check representative values, units, signs, conditions and limiting cases against the accompanying explanation. State relevant boundary handling, display clipping and approximations; do not pass a visual artifact off as a physical result. Generated illustrations may establish a setting, but are not numerical evidence. Preserve source/version and authored/adapted identity for text and media; rewriting or redrawing does not itself establish redistribution rights.

Provide readable local inputs and a text, table or static-figure alternative when interaction, motion, sound or a video cannot be used. In a plain Markdown vault, an explanation with computed examples may be the appropriate implementation; do not claim a live interactive feature that the host does not provide.

For bilingual delivery, keep concept and activity identities aligned across languages. Check the question, explanation, hints, solution, figure labels, axes, controls, feedback, captions and relevant transcript. Preserve mathematical meaning, notation and units. Changing language should preserve the task and learning position without revealing answers or granting a new independent attempt. Label untranslated or unavailable resources rather than implying complete bilingual coverage.

## Verify the capability, not just the experience

Include a changed condition, counterexample or new decision that tests the stated objective, with enough local data to attempt it. Check course notation and exam-relevant work where that is part of the learner's goal. Keep interest feedback, conceptual explanation, supported correction, independent performance and later retention distinct. Showing an answer invalidates an independence claim about a subsequent correction on that same item; use a new task when independence matters.

Record only genuine learner activity in its existing authoritative workflow, following [learning-records.md](learning-records.md). A design review, generated demonstration or test fixture is not a learner attempt. These rules do not authorize a second profile database or copying evidence between the Skill's record and a product's store; preserve the existing ownership and correction rules. Content checks establish correctness and usability within their scope, not motivational benefit or general teaching effectiveness.
