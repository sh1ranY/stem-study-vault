# Review actual teaching output

Use the sources and generated lessons, not only the skill instructions, headings or the agent's self-report. Record exact passages/locations for findings. A completed checklist without inspecting the output is not an evaluation.

## Acceptance questions

| Dimension | What the reviewer needs to observe | Material failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source coverage | Every source section accounted for; substantive concepts, conditions, visuals, examples and subparts actually taught or explicitly blocked | A topic is merely named; a distinct example or subpart disappears |
| Self-study sufficiency | A learner with the stated background can follow the actual model, notation and prerequisites | Essential reasoning is delegated to absent prior notes or source pages |
| Justified derivation | Starting relation, non-obvious transformations, domain and relevant optimality/validity check explained | Results jump from “optimize” to an unexplained equation |
| Conceptual meaning | Prose explains what is fixed, why the construction exists and how the result answers the question | Symbol manipulation without an interpretable connection |
| Narrative clarity | Connected paragraphs develop ideas; summaries and lists have an appropriate supporting role | A whole lecture is a few bullets plus one calculation; filler adds length without substance |
| Examples and practice | Distinct supplied cases are preserved; questions have all givens/subparts; independent practice follows teaching | One generic worked example replaces the other source cases |
| Mathematical/visual validity | Recomputed results, conditions, figure coordinates and labels agree; representative math renders | Correct-looking but wrong formulas, swapped effects or mislabeled curves |
| Scope honesty | Complete versus pending work is accurate; content review is separate from learner mastery | Empty indices or mapped topics are presented as a completed taught course |

A material failure requires repair or an explicit incomplete outcome; do not average it away with a high overall score. Avoid word-count thresholds: a long lesson can still omit the crucial argument.

## Specific reviewer probes for the original three-lecture fixture

The input pack contains 16 slide sections, one administrative. Inspect the teaching for the other 15 sections and the 10 requested subparts across P1–P3. These probes supplement, not replace, reading the full sources:

- F01: budget meaning and geometry; why the xy optimum is interior and budget-binding; a derived optimum with a maximum check; meaning of MRS; the distinct linear-utility corner example; the worked example and both price/income-scaling comparisons. The corner example cannot be reduced to a warning saying “corners exist.”
- F02: the fixed-input distinction between ordinary and compensated demand; expenditure derivation with a minimum check; full A/B/C example with both coordinates; graph and transition interpretation; old-bundle affordability versus old-utility compensation with their different numerical values; normal-good assumption and direction caveat.
- F03: both ordinary and compensated elasticities, each with the right fixed variables; signed versus absolute convention; point response versus an exact finite change; full P1(a–c), P2(a–d), P3(a–c) reasoning and answers.

Check concrete numerical anchors independently: F01 slide 4 uses M=40 and prices (2,1); F02 slide 3 uses M=32 and price change (1,1) to (4,1); P2 uses M=30 and price change (1,1) to (9,1). Do not give these review conclusions to the generating agent in advance.

Report the actual host/model if known, inputs and output artifacts, what was inspected, substantial findings and limitations. Success on a small synthetic course is not measured learning improvement or proof of quality on every real course.
